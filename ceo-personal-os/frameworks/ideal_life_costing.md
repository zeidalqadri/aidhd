# Ideal Lifestyle Costing

*Adapted from Tim Ferriss's concept of "Lifestyle Design" and "Dreamlining." The core insight: most people never calculate what their ideal life actually costs — and it's usually less than they think.*

---

## Overview

Before you can design your ideal life, you need to know what it costs. Most people have a vague sense that they need "more" — more money, more success, more achievement — without ever calculating the specific number.

This exercise makes it concrete. You'll design your ideal lifestyle, price it out, and discover two things:

1. The specific monthly income that would fund your dreams
2. The gap between that number and where you are now

Often, the gap is smaller than expected. Sometimes, your ideal life costs less than your current one.

---

## When to Use This

- During annual planning
- When considering a major career or business decision
- When feeling trapped by financial pressure
- When lifestyle creep has you earning more but enjoying less

**Time required:** 1-2 hours

## Key Insights from This Exercise

Most people discover:

1. **The gap is smaller than imagined.** "I need to be rich" becomes "I need $X more per month."

2. **The biggest costs aren't what they expected.** Often it's housing or taxes, not lifestyle.

3. **There's a lot of fat in current spending.** Money going to things that don't actually increase happiness.

4. **The "minimum viable dream" is surprisingly achievable.** Sometimes it's already funded by current income, just poorly allocated.

5. **Time and flexibility are expensive.** Freedom often costs more than stuff.

---

## Updating This Exercise

Revisit annually during your annual review. As your life changes, so will the costs:

- Kids grow up
- Priorities shift
- "Essential" expenses become optional
- New dreams emerge

Keep the old versions for comparison. Interesting to see what changes.

---

## A Warning

This exercise can be used to justify endless striving ("I need to earn more to reach my number") or endless delay ("I can't live my ideal life until I hit my number").

That's missing the point.

The point is clarity. Once you know the number:

- You can make rational tradeoffs
- You can spot lifestyle creep before it happens
- You can recognize when you've already won
- You can stop chasing vague "more"

The ideal life isn't waiting at a specific income level. It's built piece by piece, starting now.
