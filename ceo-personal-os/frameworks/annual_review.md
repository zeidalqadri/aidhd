# Annual Review Framework

*Adapted from Dr. Anthony Gustin's Annual Review process. His original framework provides a structured, evidence-based approach to yearly reflection.*

---

## Overview

The annual review is not about judging yourself. It's about seeing clearly what actually happened, extracting lessons, and using that clarity to design the next year with intention.

Most people skip this. They roll from one year to the next without pausing to understand what worked, what didn't, and why. Then they repeat the same patterns.

You're not most people.

---

## When to Use This

- Last week of December or first week of January
- After a major life transition (new role, exit, divorce, health event)
- Whenever you feel you've drifted significantly

**Time required:** 3-4 hours, can be split across sessions

---

## The Five Phases

### Phase 1: Data Gathering

Before you can reflect, you need data. Spend 30-60 minutes gathering:

**Calendar Review**
- Export your calendar for the year
- Note major events, trips, milestones
- Identify patterns: what consumed your time?

**Review Past Check-ins**
- Read through daily and weekly reviews
- Note recurring themes, wins, frustrations
- Look for patterns you didn't see in the moment

**Review Goals**
- Pull up last year's 1-year goals
- What did you actually accomplish?
- What did you abandon? Why?

**External Feedback**
- Any performance reviews, 360 feedback, or direct feedback received?
- What did others see that you might have missed?

## After You Finish

1. Transfer key insights to `memory.md`
2. Save this completed review to `reviews/annual/[YEAR].md`
3. Use insights to inform your goal-setting for the coming year
4. Schedule your Q1 review on your calendar

---

## A Note on Self-Compassion

This review asks hard questions. You'll uncover failures, missed opportunities, and ways you fell short.

That's the point — you can't improve what you don't see.

But don't use this as ammunition against yourself. Every successful person has a list of failures. The difference is they learned from them.

Be honest, but be kind. You're doing the work that matters.
